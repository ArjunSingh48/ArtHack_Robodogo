## Short description (what it is, why you made it)

Real good boy is an art and robotics prototype exploring whether a robot dog can use art as a guide to connecting with humans through body language.

Real dogs have evolved alongside us over thousands of years. Our connection draws on instinct, learning, and a shared vocabulary of gestures: they read our bodies and respond with their own. Robot dogs have no such inherited relationship. Their programmed behavior can make them feel less alive, and less easy to connect with.

We made Real good boy to ask: what if a robot dog's behavior could draw on the history of human–dog companionship, rather than only on explicitly programmed routines? Art preserves traces of that relationship—how humans and dogs stand, lean, look, and reach toward one another. Our prototype explores this idea by matching human body language to images of human–dog relationships and using the depicted dog's posture to guide a supported robot response. It is a small experiment in giving a robot dog a cultural source of reference for becoming a real good boy.
